A rate request is priced independently against every courier whose rate card covers the route.
Each produces its own quote, so the response is a list to choose from rather than a single number.
A courier that does not serve the destination contributes nothing.

## What a quote is built from

Every quote resolves the same five inputs against the courier's rate card, in this order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Base rate">
    A flat starting price on the rate card for the route's service level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weight surcharge">
    Each card includes an allowance. Weight above it is charged per kilogram, and any weight
    bracket the parcel falls into adds its own surcharge on top.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Zone multiplier and flat addon">
    The origin and destination resolve to named zones, and the pair selects a multiplier and a
    flat addon. When both ends match a zone the courier has priced explicitly, that entry wins
    over a partial match.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Service level">
    A multiplier on the subtotal: economy `0.9`, standard `1.0`, same day `1.2`, overnight
    `1.3`, express `1.5`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Distance overage">
    Added for a door delivery outside the courier's free allowance. Covered in full below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The courier's subtotal is `(base + weight surcharge + zone addon) × zone multiplier × service
multiplier + distance overage`, plus any special surcharges the request asked for — a fragile or
insurance flag, for instance, each either a fixed amount or a percentage of the running subtotal.

The platform commission is added to that subtotal to produce `total_price`. The `breakdown` object
on every quote carries each component separately, so a quote can be reconciled line by line
against the total it claims.

## Zone blocks and the distance overage

A courier draws its service areas as polygons on a map — a zone is a named area with a real
boundary, not a radius from a depot. A door delivery is priced by how far the destination falls
**from the boundary of the zone**, not from its centre or from the courier's office.

Each zone carries three numbers: a free allowance in metres, a step size in kilometres, and a
price per step. Distance up to the allowance costs nothing extra. Beyond it, the overage is billed
in **whole steps**, rounded up:

```
overage = ceil((distance_from_boundary − free_allowance) ÷ step_size) × step_price
```

A destination 2.4 km past a zone whose allowance is 500 m, with 1 km steps at 80 each, is
`ceil((2400 − 500) ÷ 1000) = 2` steps, so 160. Crossing into a new step is what changes the price,
which means a delivery 1.1 km out and one 1.9 km out cost the same.

Two cases resolve differently. A destination **inside** a service area is priced against that
area, so it pays nothing beyond the allowance. A destination **outside every area** is matched to
the nearest zone that will extend delivery there, and charged from that zone's edge.

<Note>
The overage applies to door delivery, and only where the courier has drawn its service areas and
priced them. A courier that has not done so quotes no overage, and a shipment routed to a pickup
point is delivered to a fixed location the courier already serves, so none arises there either.
In every case the amount charged is on the quote as `breakdown.distance_overage`, including when
it is zero.
</Note>

## What a quote commits to

A quote is a firm price with an expiry. Each carries a `quote_id`, a `valid_until` timestamp, and
the full breakdown. Creating a shipment consumes the quote, and a quote can be consumed once —
the price that was quoted is the price the shipment is created at, even if the underlying rate
card changes in between.

A quote that has expired or has already been used is refused rather than silently repriced, so a
shipment never costs more than the quote a customer was shown.

## On-demand fares

The on-demand pool prices differently: an independent driver is paid a distance fare rather than a
courier's rate card. Where a corridor has a published flat fare — a fixed price between two named
cities, valid up to a maximum distance — that fare is used instead of the distance calculation.
The mechanics of matching a driver are covered in [Dispatch & Fulfilment](/tldp/how-tldp-works/dispatch).
